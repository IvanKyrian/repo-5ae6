Summary

Bottom line
  1. USD3 is mostly an SMB trade-credit book. 80% of backing is two forward-flow books that 3Jane owns outright: Slope (56%) and Settle (24%). Neither has an overcollateralization cushion. LendSwift, the scary-looking consumer line, is only 5.7% of backing and the best protected.
  2. USD3 principal is well protected. USD3 yield is not. Losses come out of pool income first (~$7.8M/yr), then sUSD3 ($8.5M). USD3 principal only takes a hit after ~$16M of losses in a year, or ~$8.5M in a sudden shock. Slope's full history (1.7% loss per 2‑month cycle) would wipe out the yield but not touch principal.
  3. Liquidity is better than we feared in July. If 3Jane stopped redeploying, ~43% of the pool would be cash within 30 days and ~66% within 60. The real liquidity risk is the ~$53M of Morpho loops against USD3 and PT‑USD3, with the PT maturing 17 Dec.
  4. LCC fees are real USDC but too small for us as built. 1.25% on the promise = 20.2% on margin. The per-address cap is $1M of promise, which is ~$15k a year. We need a bespoke allocation for it to matter.
  5. Looping does not clear the hurdle on organic yield. The live USD3/USDC Morpho market borrows at 6.43% against USD3 at 6.30%, which is negative carry. Looping only works with cheap borrowing (≤ ~5.2% for a 12% hurdle) or JANE. That applies to looping now and to looping a call.

Recommendation going into the call: the opportunity worth pursuing is LCC with a bespoke cap, sized as a 1.25% overlay on liquidity we hold anyway. We should be comfortable holding USD3 for 35 days if called. Don't underwrite the loop at today's borrow rates, and treat JANE as upside. Before IC we need: (a) why Slope is in amortization and what its repurchase and trigger terms are, (b) the USD3l Morpho market terms, (c) the LendSwift participation and lien chain.

What backs USD3

Depositors put in USDC and get USD3 (senior) or sUSD3 (junior). Both sit on top of one pool of assets. sUSD3 absorbs losses first and earns more; USD3 is paid first and loses last. Both tranches take a fixed share of whatever the pool earns, so if income drops, USD3's yield drops too. Seniority protects USD3's principal, not its coupon.

The three credit lines

In all three, the credit risk is the end borrowers (businesses or consumers), not the fintech itself. The fintech still matters a lot, because it underwrites, services and collects, and in two of three cases stands behind repurchase promises.

Slope · SMB trade credit · forward flow

56% of backingAmortizing · $0 capacity0.00% NCO to date

What it is. B2B "buy now, pay later". A business buys inventory, Slope pays the supplier, and the business repays in 30–90 days. Slope's partners include Amazon (sellers), Samsung and IKEA business checkout. 3Jane buys the loans outright into its own SPV (true sale). Slope keeps servicing them for 0.5%.

How 3Jane is protected. Loans must pass an eligibility box at purchase: >12 months in business, >$1M revenue, SBFE tier ≤4, size caps of $350k (T≤3) / $200k (T4), no EPD, plus Slope's reps and repurchase. After that it's just the ~16% APR and then sUSD3. There is no OC layer. The app lists the trigger schedule and R&W repurchase as "Pending data".

The real risk. Losses come from a few big names, not from the crowd. In Slope's own tape, the top 20 obligors caused 81% of all losses, and one manufacturer lost $2.9M (37.5% of its exposure). Recoveries were ~0%. Remove three obligors and lifetime losses drop from 1.72% to 0.27%.

Settle · inventory or payables finance · back-book + forward flow

24% of backingDeploying0.00% NCO to date

What it is. Settle pays an e-commerce or DTC brand's supplier invoice, and the brand repays over 30–180 days in 30‑day fee blocks (54% single payment at maturity, 46% amortizing). 3Jane bought Settle's seasoned back-book (~$14.8M, par + 0.40%) in September and keeps buying new loans under a forward flow of up to $25M. Settle has originated $3.8B since 2019.

How 3Jane is protected. No OC. The protection is contractual. Settle represents that no obligor is delinquent or in forbearance anywhere in Settle's book. Any breach or first-payment default triggers mandatory repurchase. Collections are remitted twice weekly, and Nelnet is backup servicer. 3Jane's own memo prices expected loss at 0.81% of balance (9.4% IRR, breakeven at 2.3%).

The real risk. Lumpy obligors (67 in the back-book, largest 3%) in tariff-sensitive e-commerce, plus single-pay principal that hasn't been tested yet. The repurchase promise is only as good as Settle. Settle Inc. has $44M of equity against $303M of warehouse debt, turned its first profitable half in H1‑26, and the seller is a subsidiary with no standalone financials.

LendSwift · subprime consumer installment · senior warehouse

5.7% of backingOC 41.4% · in range31% of balance 31+ DPD

What it is. Payday-style installment loans: ~$500, ~4 months, ~690% APR, to underserved US consumers. 3Jane does not own these loans. It holds a senior secured participation in a loan to LendSwift's SPV, advanced at 75% of eligible collateral and paying a 15% coupon. The line is $10M, revolving until May 2027 with final maturity Nov 2027.

How 3Jane is protected. Eligible collateral must stay at least 1.33× what's owed, tested weekly. Delinquent loans are haircut out first: anything >45 DPD gets zero credit, 30–45 DPD gets 60%, and a last payment under 50% of schedule gets zero. All collections go to a DACA account. A breach stops recycling and sweeps cash to 3Jane.

Why the high DPD isn't the issue. At 690% APR the pool is priced for many borrowers never paying in full. Seasoned vintages collect 1.5× the cash lent, and the pool is back to 100% by month 3–4. The issues are elsewhere: LendSwift itself (the backup servicer is "cold"), regulation (59% of balance in Texas), and the legal chain (see risks).

How much has to default?

Losses flow in a fixed order: pool income → sUSD3 → USD3. The chart puts five 12‑month scenarios on that ladder. The scenarios are my calibration from the data room, assuming today's mix of loans stays roughly the same. Use them to frame the discussion, not as a forecast.

Where the run risk actually sits

About $53M of USDC is borrowed on Morpho against USD3 or PT‑USD3: $10.2M in USD3/USDC (LLTV 91.5%, 6.43% borrow) and $43.3M across three PT‑USD3‑17DEC2026 markets. That's roughly half of USD3, and it's levered, much of it for JANE.

The PT matures on 17 Dec. Loopers have to roll or unwind, which could bring a wave of USD3 redemptions. Slope's scheduled $22.5M of December repayments would cover a lot of it. Ask if that's by design.

USD3 redeems only "up to the tranche ratio" and subject to cash. sUSD3 has a 1‑month lock, then cooldown. Committed liquidity facilities are still on the roadmap.

LCC: what we'd actually earn

Mechanics, with $1M. Post $75k margin (7.5%) and promise $1M. You earn 1.25% × $1M = $12.5k a year from 3Jane, plus Aave on the $75k (~$2.6k): 20.2% on margin. The app's 41.6% also counts JANE at an assumed $250M FDV. 3Jane pays the fee out of pool interest. It's cheaper for them than holding idle cash that earns less than USD3 pays.

On a call (pro-rata, at most once per 21‑day epoch) you have 9 days to send USDC. You get USD3, not sUSD3, locked for 35 days, with +0.75% APR during the lock. A matching slice of margin is released. Miss the call and your whole margin goes to the backstop auction. You get back what the auction doesn't need, and nothing if no bidder fills it.

The loop uses one atomic transaction. Flash-borrow, fund the call, post the USD3l on Morpho, and borrow up to 84% LTV (liquidation at 86%). You only need 16% of the promise as capital. To promise 6.25× your net worth, the wallet must show that net worth in verifiable DeFi assets.

Key risks, ranked

1. Slope concentration and opacity. 56% of backing, owned outright, no OC. Repurchase and trigger terms aren't public, and the book is in amortization for reasons we don't know. Historical losses came from a few names with ~0% recovery. I don't have the Slope sale agreement. Ask for it.

2. Thin junior. sUSD3 is 7.4% of the pool vs the 15% used in 3Jane's own loss example. A sudden loss of 8.6% of the FCC book reaches USD3.

3. Leverage and liquidity loop. ~$53M of Morpho debt against USD3 and PT‑USD3, sized to JANE farming. If JANE disappoints at the final mint or borrow rates spike, deleveraging turns into USD3 redemptions. The PT matures on 17 Dec.

4. LendSwift legal chain. The SPE's LLC agreement only imposes separateness "so long as" it's party to a Participation Agreement with Series 78 of Cadence Group (Percent). That fits Jeremy's description of a "participation", so 3Jane may sit behind Percent as lender of record. The LLC agreement also has no independent manager, no limit on a voluntary bankruptcy filing, and the member can dissolve it by written consent. Bankruptcy remoteness has to come from documents we haven't seen.

5. Originator and servicer failure. LendSwift has only a cold backup servicer, and at 690% APR collections depend on active servicing. Settle is thinly capitalized ($44M equity vs $303M warehouse debt). Its repurchase and EPD protections are worth as much as Settle Funding LLC, which has no standalone financials.

6. Regulatory. LendSwift charges ~690% APR with 59% of balance in Texas. A state action could impair collections quickly. Ask about the licensing model.

7. Governance. 3Jane can change the USD3/sUSD3 income split and controls call timing and size. It can pause LCC with no time limit and revoke positions. Upgrades go through a 7‑day timelock. LCC's leveraged helper was audited by Guardian on 27 Oct. The core was audited by Veridise, Sherlock and Electisec.

Mitigants that are real: very short assets (2‑month WA remaining term), weekly surveillance, granular LendSwift pool with strong OC, live loan-level transparency, independent proof of reserves (Accountable), bank-held cash (Erebor), and a carefully done Settle diligence memo.
